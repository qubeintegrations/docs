---
title: "Get QuickBooks for testing"
description: "Intuit gives developers a free not-for-resale license for QuickBooks Desktop and Enterprise. Here's how to set up a test machine."
icon: "desktop"
---

[Simulated connections](/simulator) cover most of your development. Before you launch, test against the
real thing once: a copy of QuickBooks Desktop on Windows, with the Web Connector, exactly as your customers
run it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Get a Windows machine">
    QuickBooks Desktop runs only on Windows. A Windows virtual machine works fine, and makes it easy to
    snapshot a clean install.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Install QuickBooks">
    Download a trial of the edition your customers use (Pro, Premier or Enterprise) and install it. The Web
    Connector comes with QuickBooks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Request a not-for-resale license">
    Intuit gives developers a free
    [Not-For-Resale license](https://developer.intuit.com/app/developer/qbdesktop/docs/get-started/not-for-resale-software),
    so you keep a working copy when the trial ends. Approval can take a few days, so request it early.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open a sample company">
    When QuickBooks starts, choose one of its sample company files. They're full of realistic customers,
    items and transactions to test against.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect it to your sandbox app">
    Create a connection in your **sandbox** app and open its onboarding link on the Windows machine
    ([Connect your customers](/onboarding) walks through it). Then turn on
    [verbose Web Connector logs](/qwc-logs) in case you need them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Run QuickBooks as the administrator, in single-user mode, the first time your app connects. QuickBooks
  asks the admin to authorize a new integration, and that dialog blocks every request until someone answers
  it.
</Tip>
