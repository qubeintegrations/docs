Testing a QuickBooks Desktop integration has usually meant a Windows VM, a licensed copy of QuickBooks, and
waiting minutes for the Web Connector on every change. Worse, the failures that matter most, such as a
duplicate name, QuickBooks closed or a stale `edit_sequence`, are hard to reproduce on purpose.

A **simulated connection** replaces all of that. It's a fake QuickBooks Desktop company file hosted by QuBe
Sync, driven by the same Web Connector session code as a real connection, so your requests, webhooks and
workflows go through the same code they will in production. It answers within seconds, and you can make it fail
however you like.

<Note>
  Simulated connections are in beta. The simulator doesn't match real QuickBooks in every case yet, and its
  answers change as it gets closer: a change that makes it more accurate isn't treated as a breaking change.
  The simulator's endpoints, its fault names and the `simulated` connection type are stable. A request it
  doesn't model is answered with status 1030 rather than a guess, and the gaps we know about are listed
  below. [Test against real QuickBooks](#then-test-against-the-real-thing) before you launch, and if an
  answer differs from what real QuickBooks says, tell us at [support@qubesync.com](mailto:support@qubesync.com).
</Note>

## Create one

```bash
curl -u "$QUBE_API_KEY:" -X POST https://qubesync.com/api/v2/connections \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"name": "Test company", "type": "simulated"}'
```

It's connected as soon as it exists. There's nothing to install and no onboarding. From the CLI:
`qube connections create --simulated --name "Test company"`. Simulated connections are never billed.

## What's in the company file

Every simulated company starts with the same small, realistic data set, so your tests can rely on it:

| Kind | Records |
| --- | --- |
| Customers | Riverbend Electric, Hillcrest Dental, Marisol Vega |
| Vendors | Lone Star Supply Co, Capital Freight |
| Items | Consulting (\$150, service), Installation (\$95, service), Shipping (\$25, non-inventory), Widget (\$40, 120 on hand), Gadget (\$129, 35 on hand), Rush Fee (\$50, other charge) |
| Accounts | Checking, Accounts Receivable, Inventory Asset, Undeposited Funds, Accounts Payable, Sales Tax Payable, Opening Balance Equity, Sales, Cost of Goods Sold, Office Supplies |
| Other lists | Terms (Net 15, Net 30, Due on receipt), sales tax codes (Tax, Non), payment methods (Check, Cash, Visa), ship methods, customer types, classes |

Customers, vendors, employees, items and transactions such as invoices, bills and payments can be queried,
added and modified. The simulator enforces QuickBooks' rules along the way: names are unique across lists
(3100), references must exist (3140), and updates need the current `edit_sequence` (3200). Values keep
their spacing as QuickBooks keeps it: a name's double space or a note's line break comes back exactly as
sent, and a `full_name` filter finds a record by that spelling, or by one with extra spaces, but a
single-spaced filter does not find a double-spaced name. Iterated queries page like the real thing.
The company, host and preferences queries answer from the company file too: its preferences are a new
file's, with account numbers, class tracking, inventory and estimates on, one currency and sales tax off,
and they can't be changed. Requests it doesn't model yet, such as reports, are answered with status 1030,
"not supported by this implementation".

Reset it to the starting data at any time:

```bash
curl -u "$QUBE_API_KEY:" -X POST "https://qubesync.com/api/v2/connections/$CONN_ID/simulator/reset"
```

## Transactions and their lines

Transactions keep their lines the way QuickBooks keeps them, so you can build and read line items
against the simulator:

- **Sales forms** (invoices, estimates, sales orders, sales receipts, credit memos) keep their item and
  item-group lines. **Purchases** (bills, checks, credit card charges and credits, item receipts, vendor
  credits) keep their `item_line_add`, `item_group_line_add` and `expense_line_add` lines. Every line gets a
  `txn_line_id`.
- An item line with no price starts from the item's: its sales price on a sale, its purchase cost on a
  purchase. A `quantity` and an `amount` without a price imply one. Otherwise `amount` is `quantity`
  times the price, with a quantity of 1 when none is given. An expense line carries the `amount` you send.
- An item-group line expands into one line per item in the group, each item's quantity times the group's.
- Totals come from the lines: an invoice's `subtotal` and `balance_remaining`, a bill's `amount_due` and
  `open_amount`, a check's or card charge's `amount`, an item receipt's `total_amount`, and a vendor
  credit's `credit_amount` and `open_amount`. A bill posts to Accounts Payable and falls due by its
  terms, or 10 days after its date.
- Queries return item, group and expense lines only with `include_line_items` (a journal entry's lines
  come back either way). Lines come back in the order QuickBooks received them.
- A line that names an item, account, customer or item group that doesn't exist is refused with 3140.
- **Updating lines** follows QuickBooks' rule: send `txn_line_id` `-1` to add a line, an existing
  `txn_line_id` to change one, and leave a line out to remove it. Item and group lines count as one list.
  A purchase's expense lines are a separate list, replaced only when you send `expense_line_mod`.
  `clear_item_lines` and `clear_expense_lines` empty a list. An update that sends no lines leaves them
  alone.
- **Journal entries** keep their debit and credit lines. `journal_line_mod` changes them by `txn_line_id`
  under the same rule, and `journal_line_type` (`Debit` or `Credit`) moves a line to the other side. A
  new line needs a type. An entry whose debits and credits don't balance is refused with 3180 when it's
  created and 3170 when it's updated, with the message "Transaction not in balance." Both codes and
  full messages match what real QuickBooks answers. An entry created with an entry-level `memo` is saved
  without it and answered with a status 530 warning (`The field "Memo" is not supported by this
  implementation.`), as real QuickBooks does; a `memo` on a line is kept.
- **Lines inside a group** can be updated too: an `invoice_line_group_mod` (or a purchase's
  `item_group_line_mod`) with its own line updates changes the group's lines by `txn_line_id` under the
  same rule, and a new group `quantity` scales its lines. Intuit doesn't document how real QuickBooks
  handles these updates. The simulator copies how a group behaves when it's added, so test group
  updates against real QuickBooks too.

## Payments, deposits, voids and custom fields

- **Payments** pay invoices down: an `applied_to_txn_add`'s `payment_amount` lowers the invoice's
  `balance_remaining` and marks it paid at zero. A `set_credit` applies a credit memo first: its
  `applied_amount` comes off the invoice's `balance_remaining` and the credit memo's `credit_remaining`.
  An `invoice.create` can apply credit memos with `set_credit` the same way. What the credit memos have
  left is the payment's `unused_credits`, and the part of a payment the invoice no longer needs is its
  `unused_payment`. Each `applied_to_txn_ret` gives the invoice's `balance_remaining` after the payment
  and lists each credit it applied as a `linked_txn` (`txn_type` `CreditMemo`, `link_type` `AMTTYPE`, a
  negative `amount`); its own `amount` is the payment's part only. Intuit doesn't document how real
  QuickBooks lists an applied credit, so don't build on the `linked_txn` shape. The simulator caps a credit at what's available and what the invoice owes, and a
  payment at what's left after the credits; real QuickBooks may refuse such a payment instead.
- **Deposits**: a payment received without a `deposit_to_account_ref` goes to Undeposited Funds, as in
  QuickBooks. `receive_payment_to_deposit.query` lists it until a deposit takes it. A `deposit.create`
  line with a `payment_txn_id` deposits that payment for its whole amount, and a line with an
  `account_ref` and `amount` deposits money from that account. `deposit_total` is the sum of the lines.
  A payment that isn't waiting to be deposited (unknown, deposited already, or received straight into a
  bank account) is refused with 3120. Deleting a deposit puts its payments back in Undeposited Funds.
- **Voids**: `txn_void.execute` keeps the transaction and sets its totals and every line's amount to 0,
  for bills, checks, credit card charges and credits, credit memos, invoices, item receipts, journal
  entries, sales receipts and vendor credits. The memo gets `VOID: ` in front, as QuickBooks Desktop
  shows it; Intuit doesn't document that, so don't build on it. An unknown `txn_id`, or one of another
  type, gets 3120.
- **Custom fields and private data extensions**: `data_ext_def.create`, `.update`, `.delete` and
  `.query` keep definitions per company, unique by `owner_id` and `data_ext_name`. Following Intuit's
  rules, a custom field (`owner_id` `0`) must be `STR255TYPE` and assigned to Customer, Employee,
  Vendor or Item, and one assigned to Customer also reaches credit memos, estimates, invoices, sales
  orders and sales receipts. To get one onto sales orders, assign it to Customer, or use a private
  extension assigned to SalesOrder; the refusal says so. A private extension (a GUID `owner_id`) can be assigned to any list or
  transaction type, and only a private definition can be deleted. `data_ext.create`, `.update` and
  `.delete` set and clear a value on a list object or a transaction whose type the definition is
  assigned to. A query that sends `owner_id` gets the values back as `data_ext_ret`, for those owners
  only. Pass `owner_id` to `data_ext_def.query` too (0 for custom fields, your GUID for private
  extensions). Without it QuickBooks answers status 1 "did not find a matching object" even when
  definitions exist, so an empty answer there doesn't prove a field is missing. The simulator answers the
  same way, and a custom field's definition comes back as real QuickBooks returns it: `data_ext_id` 0,
  `data_ext_format_string` "Any text", and `assign_to_object` listing the transactions a Customer field
  reaches. Refusals of what Intuit rules out (a custom field on a sales order, a value with no definition)
  use 3180, 3170, 3100 and 3120; Intuit doesn't document those codes, so treat them as approximate.
  An unknown `txn_id` gets 3120, as real QuickBooks answers it.

The simulator doesn't model sales tax (always 0), payments against bills (bills stay open), inventory
quantities or account balances moving, units of measure, linked transactions such as `link_to_txn_id`
(only a payment's `applied_to_txn_ret` lists the credits it applied), price levels, account types (a card charge
can name any account), date-driven terms, payment discounts, `is_auto_apply` (the payment is received
unapplied), changing what an update to an invoice or payment applies, sales receipts waiting in
Undeposited Funds, data extension values' types and required flags, or QuickBooks refusing a
transaction with no lines. It answers 1030 rather than guess for: voiding a deposit, bill payment,
statement charge, credit card refund or inventory adjustment, a transaction that's already void, or one
with payments or credits applied to it; a data extension on a transaction line or on the company; and
a deposit with cash back.

Other differences we know of:

- A reference by a `list_id` that doesn't exist gets 3140. Real QuickBooks answers 3120 for an id, and 3140
  only for a `full_name`.
- Deleting a record that's in use, such as a customer with invoices, succeeds.
- Queries for deleted records always find nothing.
- A payment applied to a `txn_id` that doesn't exist, or a `set_credit` naming a `credit_txn_id`
  that isn't a credit memo, skips it without an error.
- Every simulated company allows every feature. A real company file can answer 3250 ("This feature is not
  enabled or not available in this version of QuickBooks") for bar codes, currencies, inventory sites or units
  of measure, and decline payroll and sales-tax-return queries.

## Make it fail

`PUT /connections/{id}/simulator` sets **faults**. Each `PUT` replaces the whole set, so send every fault
you want armed at once. Resetting clears them all.

```bash
curl -u "$QUBE_API_KEY:" -X PUT "https://qubesync.com/api/v2/connections/$CONN_ID/simulator" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"faults": {"connection_error": "modal_dialog"}}'
```

| Fault | Values | Behaves like | Lasts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `connection_error` | `qb_closed`, `modal_dialog`, `company_file_mismatch`, `unexpected` | QuickBooks not running, a dialog open, the wrong company file open, or an unexplained failure. Requests go `retryable`. | Until a `PUT` leaves it out |
| `next_status` | `{"code": 3100}`, or any QuickBooks status code, with an optional `message` | QuickBooks refusing the next request with that code. | One request |
| `next_response_error` | `"xml_error"`, or `{"hresult", "message"}` | QuickBooks failing to parse the next request. | One request |
| `next_lost_response` | `true` | QuickBooks applying the next request, whatever it is, then the Web Connector dying before it answers. The request ends `timed_out`; a workflow that sent it [settles it](/workflows#lost-answers). | One request |
| `latency_ms` | `0` to `60000` | QuickBooks taking its time over each answer. | Until a `PUT` leaves it out |

`GET /connections/{id}/simulator` shows the current faults and a summary of the company file, and
`POST /connections/{id}/simulator/sync` runs the fake Web Connector right away, like clicking **Update
Selected**.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="CLI">
    ```bash
    qube simulator faults <connection> --next 3100      # the next request: name already in use
    qube simulator faults <connection> --next lost      # the next request: applied, then its answer is lost
    qube simulator faults <connection> --qb closed      # QuickBooks closed until you say otherwise
    qube simulator faults <connection> --qb ok          # QuickBooks is back
    qube simulator faults <connection> --latency 5000   # five seconds per answer
    qube simulator sync <connection>                    # run the Web Connector now
    qube simulator reset <connection>
    ```

    `--next lost` arms `next_lost_response`, and `--next ok` clears whichever next-request fault is armed.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Dashboard">
    A simulated connection's page has a **Simulator** panel: choose whether QuickBooks is reachable, what
    happens to the **Next request** (a lost answer, or 3100, 3120, 3140, 3180, 3200 or malformed XML) and how
    long QuickBooks thinks (up to 15 seconds). Run the Web Connector now, or reset the company file, and
    watch each request get queued and answered live. Other codes and latencies are set through the API.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## A test plan for your integration

Walk every path before your customers do:

| Scenario | Set up | Your app should |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Happy path | nothing | Store the returned `list_id` / `txn_id` |
| Duplicate name | `next_status: {code: 3100}` | Ask the user, or use [`create_customer_safely`](/workflows) |
| Record changed in QuickBooks | `next_status: {code: 3200}` | Re-query, then retry the update. (A workflow's update reads the record back, finds it unchanged and sends it again.) |
| Answer lost | `next_lost_response: true` | Query before resending a create or update. A workflow [settles it](/workflows#lost-answers) itself |
| Record changed before a workflow's update | A chart of your own with an update state (no template sends one): change the record through the API, then run the update with the `edit_sequence` from before | Answer the [conflict prompt](/workflows#conflicts): `overwrite` or `keep` |
| Missing reference | `next_status: {code: 3140}` | Create the referenced record, or tell the user |
| QuickBooks closed | `connection_error: qb_closed` | Show `error.user_message`, not resend; the answer follows once you clear the fault |
| Wrong company file | `connection_error: company_file_mismatch` | Show `error.user_message` |
| Slow QuickBooks | `latency_ms: 20000` | Keep your UI responsive while it waits |
| Large import | `iterator=true&max_returned=2` | Handle several pages, out of order |
| Your webhook endpoint down | stop your server, then queue a request | Recover from QuBe Sync's retries and not process a repeat twice |

## Then test against the real thing

The simulator models QuickBooks' behavior, not every corner of it. Before you launch, run your integration
against a real copy of QuickBooks Desktop: Intuit gives developers a free
[not-for-resale license](/nfr-quickbooks).

<Note>
  In a sandbox app, an [iterated query](/pagination) stops after 4 pages, with `iteration_state`
  `max_sandbox_iterations_reached`, on simulated and real connections alike.
</Note>
